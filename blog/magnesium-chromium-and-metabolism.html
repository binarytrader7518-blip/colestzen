Magnesium, Chromium and Zinc: Minerals and Metabolism

What magnesium, chromium and zinc do in the body, where to find them in food, and what NIH fact sheets say about supplements.

Published 2026-11-25 · ColestZen editorial team

Minerals do quiet but essential work in metabolism. Three of them, magnesium, chromium and zinc, are among the nine micronutrients the seller lists in ColestZen.

Magnesium

Magnesium is involved in hundreds of enzyme reactions, including those that turn food into energy. Leafy greens, nuts, seeds, legumes and whole grains are good sources. The NIH magnesium fact sheet covers recommended intakes and medicine interactions.

Chromium

Chromium helps insulin do its job. The NIH chromium fact sheet describes mixed research results on blood sugar and lipids, and notes that diabetes guidelines do not recommend chromium supplements.

Zinc

Zinc supports immune function, wound healing and many enzymes. Meat, shellfish, legumes and seeds provide it. Too much zinc over time can interfere with copper absorption, according to the NIH zinc fact sheet.

Food first

A varied diet is the main source of these minerals. If you take a supplement, count all the sources you use so you stay within safe upper limits, and tell your doctor what you take. Our nutrient focus area lists all nine micronutrients in the formula.

This article is for general education and is not medical advice. Talk with your doctor about your own health.
