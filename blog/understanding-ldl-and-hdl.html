Understanding LDL and HDL Cholesterol

What LDL and HDL cholesterol are, why the balance matters for heart health, and how routine testing and daily habits fit together.

Published 2026-11-25 · ColestZen editorial team

Cholesterol is often described as something to avoid, but your body needs it. The liver makes most of it, and it is used to build cell membranes, make hormones and produce vitamin D. The problems start when there is more of the wrong kind circulating in the blood than the body can handle.

Two carriers, two roles

Cholesterol does not travel alone. It is carried through the bloodstream inside particles called lipoproteins. LDL (low-density lipoprotein) delivers cholesterol to tissues. When there is too much LDL, it can contribute to plaque building up in artery walls, which is why it is often called the "bad" cholesterol. HDL (high-density lipoprotein) helps carry extra cholesterol back to the liver, which is why it is often called the "good" cholesterol.

Why the numbers are checked

High LDL usually causes no symptoms, so a blood test called a lipid panel is the only way to know where you stand. The CDC suggests asking your health care team how often you should be tested, because the answer depends on your age, family history and other risk factors.

What influences your levels

  • Diet: saturated and trans fats tend to raise LDL.
  • Activity: regular movement supports healthier levels.
  • Weight: carrying extra weight can push LDL up and HDL down.
  • Genetics: some people inherit a tendency toward high cholesterol, whatever their habits.

Where supplements fit

Supplements such as ColestZen are positioned as part of a daily routine alongside healthy eating, activity and regular check-ups, not as a replacement for any of them. If you have high cholesterol, your doctor's plan comes first, and any supplement should be discussed with them. Key takeaway: LDL and HDL do different jobs, high LDL is silent, and testing plus lifestyle are the foundations. Supplements are an extra, not a substitute.
